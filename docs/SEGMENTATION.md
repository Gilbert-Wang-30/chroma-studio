# Segmentation research, round 3: what is wrong, what was measured, what to ship

> **Local-only sources.** Paths under `scratch/` (the harness `scratch/segexp/evalkit.py`, the reference sets under `scratch/segexp/sets/` and `scratch/datasets/`, the diagnosis sheets under `scratch/segexp/diag/`, every experiment's `README.md`, `result.json` and `run_metrics.py` under `scratch/segexp/exp/<key>/` and `scratch/experiments/seg3/<key>/`) are the author's working files. `scratch/` is git-ignored, so the numbers that cite them can only be recomputed on the machine they were measured on. The human comparison sheets are copied into `docs/comparison/segmentation/`.

Companion to [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md) (the round-1 survey) and [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md) (the engine experiments). Date 2026-09-24. Code: the working tree after the round-3 integration, uncommitted on top of `8dc9826`; `.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/ -q` passes 264 tests in about 6 s. The round had four parts: a diagnosis of the shipping segmentation on 23 images at three presets, a harness with four reference sets, nine experiments (six segmentation lanes, three grouping lanes) and three visual judges over human comparison sheets. Every experiment number below is one an independent verifier reproduced from the saved predictions with the project `.venv`; where a verifier corrected a lane's claim the corrected reading is used. No experiment was refuted; two carry a *major* correction (`proposals`, `group_shiny`). The diagnosis numbers are proxies without ground truth, from a frozen copy of the committed package whose partitions match the fixture jobs' `labels.npy` at 0.998-1.000. Nothing tracked was modified by the research and the six fixture jobs were only read.

The owner's brief for this round, verbatim: "the grouping is problematic: when lighting is applied, even the same colour is usually bright = one group, dark = one group or more; it's not considering lighting physics. Also when a shiny part is really shiny it usually gets grouped to white. Also there isn't an option to ignore the background." Those are failure modes S4, S5 and S3 below. The segmentation lanes attack S1, S2 and S6-S13, which are what the reference sets can measure; the grouping lanes attack the three the owner sees.

## 1. What is wrong today

**The code as it stands.** The frozen copy the harness scores is `8dc9826`. The working tree adds, in `recolor/segmentation/hierarchy.py`, step 5 (`_resplit`, `RESPLIT_ROUNDS` 3) and `recover_parts` (SAM point prompts on chromatic pockets and on off-colour pieces of coloured regions, sources `'prompt'` / `'part'`), and in `refine.py` the groups-stage refinement (`absorb_washed`, `isolate_parts` / `lock_parts`, `split_decals`, `matting.snap_labels` with ViTMatte, `lock_materials`, `protect_mask`, `regroup_refined`). On the references that is worth achR 0.753 -> 0.770 and thin recall 0.211 -> 0.316 on ours (`seg3_wt_balanced_refined`) and nothing on the external sets. Diagnosis medians at a 1536 px long side, fast / balanced / max: 92 / 225 / 504 masks, 66 / 130 / 226 regions, 13 / 16 / 18 groups, SAM 0.8 / 5.4 / 19.2 s, total 1.8 / 6.5 / 20.6 s, peak VRAM 4.7 / 6.5 / 7.1 GB.

**S4 One paint split into several groups by lighting** (the first complaint). Grouping is `grouping.group_regions` -> `_cluster_regions` -> `cluster_colors`: area-weighted centroid linkage, plain CIEDE2000 at `delta_e` 10, on each region's median Careaga albedo (`labelops.region_medians`). The albedo keeps residual shading and environment reflections, so the lit, shadowed and reflecting pieces of one paint differ by more than dE 10 and stay apart; `delta_e` is the only lever (6 / 10 / 14 / 20 give 35 / 16 / 8 / 4 groups, median). The working tree's only counter-rule is `refine.absorb_washed` (a *lighter*, less saturated piece within `ABSORB_HUE_TOL` 12 deg of a chromatic group, chroma 0.5-0.95 of it, corr(L, C) <= -0.2 joins it); the darker, shaded piece has no rule. RX-78 red = Rose / Cherry / Maroon (main-paint share 0.531); its uniform black backdrop = 'Gray' L 51 above the horizon and 'Black' L 14 below, with a seam across smooth black; the Jaguar's navy over Off-white (sky reflection, albedo L 89-92) / Gray / Graphite; the owner's panda's white fur into three greys (L 81 / 59 / 44). The integration check on a fresh BMW analysis at Balanced reproduces the complaint exactly: Amber (8 regions, 7.5 %) plus Bronze (1 region, 0.11 %: the shaded inner face of the upper fairing under the mirror stalk), so painting Amber leaves a yellow patch and the pill needs Ctrl-click on both. Frequency: a hue family split into two or more groups in 17 / 19 / 19 of 23 images; a median 16 / 20 / 26 % of chromatic area outside its family's largest group; group boundaries across smooth surfaces (no photo or albedo edge within 4 px) 13-19 % of group-boundary pixels on the RX-78. Effect: "paint the red" hits part of the red, and adjacent fragments get different lightness anchors in `engine.py`, which draws seams through panels.

**S5 Groups mix materials; shiny paint and chrome land in white** (the second complaint). The same albedo-only linkage: a broad specular pushes the albedo of glossy paint toward white, so highlight-washed regions cluster with the white parts and stay unpainted; chrome, glass and lit rubber take their albedo from what they reflect. `Region` and `ColorGroup` carry only the median albedo, no photo appearance, specular or material cue. car_classic_2 'Gray' (44 % of the image) holds the front wings, the chrome bumper (photo L 8, albedo L 53), the grille and the headlights; the BMW fixture's region 60 (the washed upper fairing, 59k px) sits in the white 'Khaki' group; the RX-78 shield facet in 'Light gray'; the Ducati front wheel in 'Khaki' with the floor highlights. Frequency: a non-background group spanning more than 45 photo-L units in 19 / 21 / 22 of 23 images, covering a median 13 / 25 / 30 % of the image. Effect: "paint the car blue" paints the chrome; the shiny zone of the paint is left white.

**S3 The background swallows object parts, or there is no background at all** (the third complaint). The backdrop's area pins its centroid, so any region within dE 10 joins it, and Careaga's albedo at photo-black pixels (L < 12) is ill-posed and mid-grey (per-image p50 of albedo L 30.5, range 11.8-49.4), so black backdrops match tyres, wheels, forks and dark plastic. `grouping._mark_background` flags at most one group, and only when it owns more than `BACKGROUND_BORDER_FRACTION` 0.35 of the border; a backdrop split over two groups gets no flag. Ducati: the rear wheel + tyre region r5 (72,273 px), the swingarm and the whole-bike leftover r0 are all in background group 'Gray' (albedo L 49 where the photo is L 0.5); bicycle_2's disc wheel in the floor group; car_red_sports_1: 35 interior regions (20 % of the image) inside the background group; no flag at all at balanced on car_red_sports_2, car_classic_2, sneakers_2, robot_toy_2, two Gunpla scenes and both streets (8 of 23; a median of 4 groups is needed to cover 90 % of the border). The product exposes only that one flag: `mapping.suggest_mapping(keep_background=True)` skips it, the Groups panel shows it as a badge that can only be *unmarked* (`web/js/panels/groups.js`), and `PATCH /api/jobs/{id}/groups/{gid}` accepts `is_background`. There is no object-versus-backdrop decision and no single switch. Effect: "keep background" locks the rear wheel; no flag means auto-mapping repaints the backdrop; painting the backdrop paints the gaps inside the object.

**S1 Region boundaries sit 2-6 px off the photo edge.** SAM decodes each mask from 256x256 logits per crop and upsamples bilinearly: about 6 work px per cell on layer 0 of a 1536 image (3.7 / 2.0 on the crop layers). Layer 0 is the only layer in Fast and supplies the large masks that own the silhouettes in every preset (`hierarchy._paint_masks` paints largest first); layer-0 masks have 47-53 % of their boundary on the edge, 33-37 % 2 px or more inside it; layer 2 masks 72 %. Duplicate rejection throws the sharper crop-layer copy away (S11), and the one snap (`_refine_edges`, `REFINE_RADIUS` 2, through `filters.refine_labels_with_guide`, which reduces the RGB guide to luminance) moves a boundary about 2 px and does little across iso-luminant edges. Median share of label-boundary pixels 2 px or more off: 29.4 / 29.2 / 28.4 % (37.7 % before the snap; `REFINE_RADIUS` 4 alone gives 23.2 %). Examples: the RX-78 backdrop mask stops 3-6 px short of the armour; the Ducati fairing and headlight rim at (318, 438). Effect: the 2-6 px rim of old colour on one side of every repainted outline, which the engine's outward coverage ramp exists to compensate.

**S2 Leftover parent regions.** `_paint_masks` never revisits a parent: every pixel of a big mask no child claims keeps the parent's id, i.e. 3-6 px ribbons around every child plus whole unproposed chunks, and the region's median is recomputed from the mixture. The Ducati whole-bike mask keeps 7 % of itself (52,094 px in 211 pieces: engine, fork, frame internals) and lands in the background 'Gray'; the RX-78's r0 keeps 4 % as a 3-6 px 'Black' ribbon between every armour part and the backdrop. Regions keeping less than half their mask: 0 / 5 / 14 per image; ribbon pixels 0.8 / 1.1 / 1.5 % of the image, about half of them in a different group than their 9x9 surroundings. Effect: a dark or light outline around a painted part, and leftover chunks that drag whole parts into the wrong group.

**S6 Small distinct parts dropped.** `_paint_masks` breaks at the first proposal below `min_px` (`REGION_PRESETS` `min_area_frac` 0.0015 / 0.0006 / 0.00025 = 2,359 / 944 / 393 px at 1536x1024); `_remove_specks` merges everything below `min_px / 4` into the nearest-albedo neighbour with no colour limit; the snap erases most of what is left. Every 'DUCATI 748' letter was proposed (masks of 319-828 px, dE 73-96 from the red) and dropped, as were the tank decal, the Sazabi's gold vent frames and 'GD' emblem, the sprue's orange decals. A median 4 / 13 / 17 distinct proposals per image (dE > 15 from their ring) have no matching region; 42 / 47 / 41 distinct specks merged away. Effect: lettering, badges and small lamps repainted in the body colour.

**S7 Thin structures missing.** The point grid (38x26 px spacing at balanced layer 0) rarely lands on a 2-6 px structure; the quality filters reject thin masks (0-11 % of thin proposals pass `pred_iou_thresh` 0.76 and `stability_score_thresh` 0.90 against 20-33 % of all); `min_area` drops a 3x200 px spoke; the 5x5 snap window changes thin pixels 3-4x more often than others. Only 31 / 38 / 45 % of thin dark structures have a label boundary within 2 px; two thirds stay unbounded even at Max. bicycle_1's disc rotor and spokes sit inside the background group; the BMW brake lines and mirror stalk are partly bounded.

**S8 Multi-material regions not split.** `_split_bimodal` needs the 2-means centroids more than `SPLIT_DELTA_E` 14 apart, a valley below `SPLIT_VALLEY` 0.5, the smaller mode above `SPLIT_MIN_FRACTION` 0.08 and both halves above `min_px`, so a decal covering 1-5 % of its part never qualifies. Ducati r5: tyre + gold rim one region (26 % impure); bicycle_1: the white 'UBE' letters inside the jersey; robot_toy_1: a tube bundle. Regions with 25 % or more of their pixels 20 dE from their median: 3 / 8 / 11 per image.

**S9 Split products are scattered mixtures.** The bimodal split assigns every pixel of a possibly disconnected region to the nearer centroid, so the minority mode collects every pixel of that colour anywhere in it: the BMW's r59 (59,228 px in 174 pieces) is the nose and upper cowl plus every blown highlight on the bike, which is why the nose was never painted with the paint. The working tree's step 5 fixes this case (`ctl_resplit`: +0.5 pt achR on ours); the per-pixel assignment stays.

**S10 SLIC fill.** Every pixel SAM did not cover goes to `_fill_unlabeled` (n = HW / 600 | 400 | 300, on a 0.9 MP downscale, nearest-upsampled, merged only below `FILL_DELTA_E` 8), which leaves 300-600 px blobs on texture with stair-stepped outlines (raggedness 1.18-1.40 against 1.08-1.11 for SAM regions). SAM leaves 19 / 7 / 3.4 % of the image uncovered (median; up to 87 / 60 / 40 % on sprue_runner_1). Fast is "SLIC with some SAM" on busy scenes.

**S11 Duplicate rejection keeps the first, not the best.** A later proposal with more than `DUPLICATE_OVERLAP` 0.85 of its pixels inside one region and a median within `DUPLICATE_DELTA_E` 4 is dropped, including the near-identical crop-layer copy with the sharper boundary and, decisively, same-colour sub-parts (the BMW's black rim inside the black wheel, its upper fairing inside the fairing, its muffler inside the machinery). Near-identical pairs summed over 23 images: 194 / 692 / 1,858; the dropped copy had the higher predicted IoU in 60-63 % of them and came from a finer crop layer in 0 / 29 / 45 %. This is why Max's 3x cost over Balanced buys almost no boundary accuracy (29.2 -> 28.4 %).

**S12 The snap is luminance-only and erases small regions.** `refine_labels_with_guide` argmaxes guided-filtered indicators at radius 2 on a luminance guide; a region narrower than the window disappears (38 / 78 / 92 regions per image lose more than half their area; the Sazabi 210, 171 of them distinct) and iso-luminant pairs improve less (38.4 -> 32.3 %) than luminant ones (31.1 -> 24.1 %). The colour solver `filters.guided_filter_color` exists in the engine and is not used here. Net positive for halos today, and one of the causes of S6 and S7.

**S13 Presets.** Fast 32 points, no crops, `min_px` 2,359: 1.8 s and unusable on busy scenes. Balanced 40 + 4 crops at 20: 6.5 s. Max 64 + 4 x 32 + 16 x 16: 20.6 s, 7.1 GB, more regions and more leftovers (5 -> 14 per image), boundaries 2 px or more off 29.2 -> 28.4 %. **S14** crop-layer seams: not observed (a negative result; the AMG drops masks whose box touches a crop edge).

## 2. How it was measured

The harness is `scratch/segexp/evalkit.py`: `evaluate(segment_fn, sets)` calls `segment_fn(image_rgb_u8, albedo_lin) -> int32 labels` once per image at the set's evaluation resolution with the frozen Careaga v2.1 albedo cached per image, records crashes without stopping, and stores every prediction (`eval/<name>/pred/`), so a verifier can rescore without re-running the model. Sweeps use quick subsets (ours 6, PACO every third image = 21, PASCAL 10, bsds500_val 20); the chosen variant runs on the full sets; BSDS500 test is run once at the end and never tuned on.

| set | images | scored parts (thin) | reference | resolution | licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ours | 6 | 178 (19) | hand-checked part references on the app's 1536 px work images: motorcycle_1 (Ducati) 35 parts, motorcycle_2 (BMW) 36, car_red_sports_1 27, gundam_rx78_rg 43, street_complex_1 28, sneakers_1 24; per part: material, thin, score, edges_inside, provenance (59 SAM-only, 92 SAM + a GrabCut band snap, 27 hand-built: seam polylines, fitted rim ellipses, colour selections) | 1536 | five Wikimedia CC images, one retailer render (test use only) |
| paco | 63 | 738 (108) | PACO-LVIS v1 part masks on COCO images, one large object per image (16 cars, 9 bicycles, 8 shoes, ...) | <= 640 | annotations from an MIT repo; 41 of 63 Flickr images NC and/or ND |
| pascal_part | 30 | 187 (4) | PASCAL-Part on VOC2010: 20 cars, 10 motorbikes; body panels merged; coarse polygons, precision meaningless | about 500x375 | research use |
| bsds500 (+ val) | 200 (+ 100) | 5-9 human segmentations per image | natural scenes, boundary benchmark | 481x321 | non-commercial research |

Metrics per image, aggregated per set (`SUMMARY_KEYS`): **asa** / **ue** (achievable segmentation accuracy and under-segmentation error, Neubert and Protzel's form); **bnd p/r/f@t** (one-sided 1-px boundaries of GT and prediction matched by distance transform at 1, 2, 4 px; precision only judges predicted edges inside a scored part or within t of a GT edge); **rv@t** (recall on the visible GT edges only, CIELAB contrast >= 3); **best IoU** (one region per part) and **achievable IoU** (the union of regions whose majority label is the part: what a perfect grouping of these regions could reach), with **recall@0.5 / 0.75** on each; **thin recall** (achievable IoU >= 0.5 on parts whose mean width is at most 1 % of the long side); **purity** (pixel-weighted material purity of regions); **regions**; **runtime** (GPU-synchronised, per image). Calibration points: SLIC at 400 px per superpixel gets ASA 0.974 and achievable recall 0.938 but F@2 0.43 and best IoU 0.16; the whole image as one region gets ASA 0.72 and zero elsewhere. So achievable recall must be read at a matched region count (a gain needing more than 1.3x the regions is restated), and "f@1 / f@2" are the honest boundary numbers.

Grouping is scored separately, because region metrics cannot see S3-S5: `bggroup` wrote `colour_families.json` (one paint = one family across its panels; gold rims, tyres, decals, chrome their own) for the 178 parts, giving family pair precision / recall / F1, group material purity, background **leakage** (scored-part pixels in background-flagged groups), backdrop **coverage** (border-connected 'other' pixels in flagged groups) and flag counts on the 23 samples; the six fixture jobs give paint fragmentation (`frag_paint`: the main-paint share of the family) and the round-1 guard pairs (known wrong merges: the BMW's reservoir, fork, caliper, gold fork tube and mirror stalk must never join the paint). Renders use the live `recolor.engine.Renderer` on the fixtures' stored layers (read only), navy `#123f9e` on the unlocked paint family and all-black on every chromatic group, scored as in `scratch/halo_eval.py` / `harness3` (halo, bleed, jaggy, rim, `forkcap_moved`; identity 0 required).

Caveats, all of which the numbers below inherit. The ours references lean on SAM (151 of 178 parts are SAM or SAM + GrabCut masks), so every lane reports by provenance and a gain must hold on the hand-built parts. White-on-white (RX-78) and black-on-black (BMW) edges are uncertain by 1-3 px; invisible edges are ignore bands (BMW rim / tyre junction, sprocket behind the spokes, the Ducati front-wheel interior), and the achievable IoU excludes ignore pixels, which is what let a wheel blob score as a rim in `smallthin`. Ours is 6 images: one part is 0.56 pt of achievable recall, and every lane tuned on the same six images it reports on; only BSDS test is held out. PACO splits some parts geometrically across one surface (11 % of its GT boundary has no visible edge); PASCAL-Part's precision is meaningless; BSDS is not the product domain and distance-transform matching is more lenient than the BSDS benchmark's one-to-one correspondence, so no number here is comparable to published ODS figures. External sets run at their native <= 640 px, where SAM sees a different scale than a 1536 px product photo. Runtimes were taken on a card shared with the owner's live app and other lanes; only paired differences are meaningful, and evalkit's `runtime_s` for cached candidates excludes SAM. The judges were told to ignore the numbers on the sheets.

### 4.1 `samdecode` (S1, S2, S11, S12)

**What was tried.** A `SAM2AutomaticMaskGenerator` subclass that keeps the survivors' raw 256x256 logits (D0 reproduces the cached proposals at IoU 1.000 per mask) and re-binarises them with a colour-guided filter (G: `sigmoid(logit)` filtered by the crop RGB at radius `k x cell`, `p2 > 0.5` in a band of +-2r around the zero crossing) or a lower layer-0 threshold (T); a copy of `build_regions` with switches: P1 near-duplicate clusters (IoU >= 0.8, keep the best `predicted_iou x stability`), P1b (apply the 0.85 / dE 4 duplicate drop only when the proposal's IoU with the host's own proposal is >= 0.8, else keep the sub-part if its new boundary carries a mean photo-Lab gradient >= tau), P2 colour-constrained re-growth of parent ribbons, P3 a colour snap with small-region protection, `REFINE_RADIUS` 0.

**What happened.** P1b is the part-recall win (ours achR 0.753 -> 0.815, hand-built best IoU 0.490 -> 0.605, PASCAL achR 0.712 -> 0.864 on the quick set) and costs precision inside parts (embossed clog zones and shading bands become sub-regions); the gate at tau 5 recovers ours f@1 / f@2 (0.714 / 0.786) at PASCAL's expense (0.818). P2 loses everywhere: the ribbons carry real boundaries (frame, cables, engine bits). P3 never beats the luminance snap on f@1; no snap at all is +0.6 pt f@2 on ours and +1.0 on PACO. T does nothing. G lifts ours f@1 0.702 -> 0.723 at equal f@2 and hurts the 640 px sets (2.5 px cells erode thin parts), so it is gated to cells >= 3 px and never runs on the external sets. `final` is 4.4 s faster than shipping (the stock generator RLE-encodes every batch; the decoder decodes only the 60-290 survivors). Downstream the decode loses: Ducati navy halo 28 -> 57 with a 60x15 px red patch under the headlight (G shrinks the child masks, the whole-bike leftover no longer splits its red part out and lands in the background group), BMW navy halo 227 -> 317; `final_nodecode` keeps the Ducati at 27 but raises BMW bleed 3,449 -> 4,410. **Verifier corrections applied.** Everything reproduces (partitions re-generated from `segment_fn`, with and without the SAM cache). G's +2.7 pt f@1 rests on six in-sample images with a 0.2 pt margin and is net negative on halo for both bikes: unproven. The BMW's own boundary F drops (f@1 -1.1, f@2 -2.1). **Verdict.** Mixed. ### 4.3 `refinenet` (S1, S2, S11, S12)

**What was tried.** CascadePSP (MIT, `segmentation-refinement` 0.6, 271 MB weights) on every proposal of >= 1 % (C1) or >= 0.25 % (C2) of the image, its 8 / 16 largest (C2k), fast mode (C3), L 600 / 1200 (C4), with guards (keep the original when IoU < 0.85 or the area ratio leaves 0.8-1.25; never thin masks), band-limited (b4 / b8) and no-growth (g) variants; C5 on every final region >= 0.25 %, re-deciding only pixels within 8 px of a boundary; a SAM-native control (384 px windows re-decoded with the mask as a logit prompt plus points); every arm with the luminance snap on and off; the S11 probes (duplicate rule off, or tested on IoU 0.85 instead of containment).

**What happened.** At the level of single masks it does what the literature pass promised: large-mask boundary pixels within 1 px of the reference edge 36 % -> 82 % on ours, and the double lines around the Ducati fairing, rear tyre and RX-78 armour are gone under C2. At the partition level the gain is small and local: ours F@1 +3.8 (target +4), F@2 +1.4 (target +3), on the SAM + GrabCut parts (+4.9 / +2.4) rather than the hand-built ones (F@2 -0.2); PACO F@2 -0.5, PASCAL R@2 -2.1, BSDS val and test -0.4, and against its own snap-off control it loses on PACO and PASCAL in every configuration (refined masks grow into gaps where SLIC regions used to carry reference edges: the scissors image -8.7 pt). C5 is neutral to slightly positive outside, mostly because the snap is off, and keeps the ribbons. Variants inside the 4 s budget gain at most 0.6 pt F@2. Downstream C2 puts the Ducati's panel edges on the seam lines; the seams become unpainted neutral regions and the navy render shows thin red lines along them (halo 28 -> 71). Side findings: duplicates tested on IoU (`dupiou85`) give achR ours 0.753 -> 0.815, PACO 0.416 -> 0.466, PASCAL 0.759 -> 0.893, BSDS val 0.498 -> 0.545 at 1.18-1.32x the regions (and lose the side_fairing); the luminance snap costs 0.6-1.8 pt F@2 on every set while buying 1.2 pt F@1 on ours.

**Verifier corrections applied.** 2,541 values reproduce to 1e-6; `segment_fn` re-run bit-exact. The S11 sentence omits the newly lost side_fairing; region counts were quoted at two stages (101 -> 89 regions stage, 103 -> 92 final); the cost is a range, 10.8-27.6 s (Ducati 10.8 s idle).

**Verdict.** Reject. **Cost.** 10.8-27.6 s per image, 4.45 GB, for +1.4 pt F@2 on ours and losses elsewhere. **What it would take to ship.** Nothing; its two side findings are covered by `samdecode`'s P1b and `REFINE_RADIUS` 0.

### 4.4 `samvote` (S2, S11, S10, S1)

**What was tried.** Atoms from the XOR of random codes of the proposals covering each pixel (5,566 raw atoms per ours image, 668 after merging atoms under 12 px); a vote on each atom pair = the weight of the proposals in the symmetric difference of their memberships (W2 = `predicted_iou x stability`, W3 = W2 x (1 + dE to the ring / 30)); a Higra average-linkage tree with an albedo veto at dE 20; cuts: horizontal at shipping's count (T1b) or max's (T1m), at a fixed altitude (T2), and the in-lane proposal-anchored cut (each proposal >= 0.025 % of the image anchors its best node at IoU >= 0.4, moved up to the highest ancestor within 0.3 of that IoU; PA1b capped at balanced's count, PA2 uncapped); a pixel-level ribbon re-assignment (rb).

**What happened.** The stated hypothesis fails: a horizontal cut cannot tell a clean single-proposal part (the BMW headlight, both calipers) from a sliver, so at shipping's count it merges them away (ours best IoU 0.626 -> 0.597, achR 0.753 -> 0.680, thin 0.158) and ribbons grow (0.88 -> 1.45 %). The PA cut at matched counts meets the ours targets (best IoU +3.7, achR +3.9, f@1 +1.4, f@2 +1.5, thin +21 pt, hand-built best IoU +12.4) and misses PACO (best IoU -0.2, achR +0.5, f@2 -0.9; on 29 % of its images the proposals run out before shipping's count). PA2 from balanced proposals matches shipping max's part scores on ours (best IoU 0.671 vs 0.661, achR 0.854 vs 0.815) at 122 regions and 6 s of SAM instead of 20. The tree does not halve ribbons; rb does (0.44 %) at -1.5 pt F@2 everywhere (ragged strips). Downstream the navy halo rises (Ducati 28 -> 173 for PA1b, 61 for PA2; BMW 227 -> 379 / 255): purer sub-regions cluster into more paint-family groups, and on the BMW a "Sand" group of specular highlights (nose, knee panel, fender) turns navy.

**Verifier corrections applied.** "Half the PACO images" is 29 %; the PA2 downstream row was omitted and is closer to shipping than PA1b; thin 0.211 -> 0.421 is 4 -> 8 of 19 parts (hand parts n = 27); 2.68 GB is the process peak, dominated by the snap.

**Verdict.** Mixed; keep `_paint_masks`. **Cost.** 0.72-0.75 s on top of SAM (shipping `build_regions` 1.13 s), Higra (CeCILL-B) or a union-find rewrite of two functions, < 0.2 GB. **What it would take to ship.** Not now. Two follow-ups worth a lane: PA2 as a cheaper stand-in for the Max preset once a grouping-side guard keeps "Sand"-type specular groups out of the paint family (the same guard `group_shiny` needs); the PA cut on product photos with that guard.

### 4.7 `group_physics` (S4; the first complaint)

**What was tried.** Per-region features from the stored layers of the six fixtures and 14 live samples: albedo Lab, log-luminance gaps of albedo, shading and photo against the main paint, residual energy fraction, photo log-chromaticity and its Finlayson projection, the photo's lightness-normalised CIELAB (a, b x 66 / (L + 16)); (b) an intrinsic light test ("the albedo gap is a leak of the shading gap"); (c) log-chromaticity; a pairwise material veto (the shipped `lock_materials` rule: candidate chroma < 0.6 of the anchor's and > 10 L darker); linkage distances with these gates; and `absorb_lit`, a second pass at group level after the unchanged shipped clustering: every chromatic group (normalised chroma >= 12, not background, not locked) joins the closest larger anchor (chroma >= 30, >= 2,000 px) whose lightness-normalised albedo is within CIEDE2000 kC = 2 `alb_tol` and whose lightness-normalised photo colour is within `pho_tol`, unless the veto blocks it; every test against the anchor's own median, no chaining. Sweeps of alb 6-10 and pho 5-7; renders; evalkit region and group maps; the full live pipeline on 14 samples.

**What happened.** The physics premise is refuted with numbers: the shading layer does not carry the lighting difference that splits a paint (RX-78 Cherry vs Rose: albedo gap -0.68 with a shading gap of +0.62, the opposite sign; Maroon -1.36 with a shading gap of 0.00), and it passes a black part read as paint in shadow (the BMW mirror stalk); residual energy is a lightness proxy. Log-chromaticity is noise on saturated paint (the blue channel sits at the noise floor). Linkage distances chain through desaturated intermediates and reach the BMW fork tube (5 guard hits for round 1's blend rule). What works is the photo's lightness-normalised chromaticity (same paint under different light 0.4-2.9 apart, other materials of the same hue 6.5-7.6) with the material veto, at group level against fixed anchors; chosen alb 8, pho 7 (alb 6 loses the BMW panel at 7.3, alb 10 admits the RX-78 near-black Salmon piece at 8.6; pho 5 blocks the red car's bonnet reflection at 6.7). Fixtures: RX-78 main-paint share 0.531 -> 0.967, Sazabi 0.941 -> 0.981, red car 0.858 -> 0.988, BMW 0.911 -> 0.942 with its painted groups 3 -> 1, Ducati and Exia unchanged; zero guard-pair merges; groups fall by 1-5; purity unchanged (0.7793 -> 0.7791); region metrics untouched. Renders: the RX-78 chest sides, feet and shield one navy instead of two blues; the BMW panel under the mirror and tail underside in the fairing's navy with the anchor-seam lightness step 4.56 -> 2.58; bleed down on BMW, RX-78, Sazabi, +2,000 px on the Ducati because the shadowed caliper mount joined the gold group and rule 7's exclusion zone moved. Live samples: RX-78 0.530 -> 0.968, robot toy 0.795 -> 0.970, red car 0.854 -> 0.989, Sazabi 0.954 -> 0.982, BMW 0.912 -> 0.925; the Zaku's two greens stay apart, but only because their chroma differs (17 vs 30). The neutral S4 case (the RX-78 backdrop, L 14 / L 51) is not addressed: no neutral pair is ever touched.

**Verifier corrections applied.** Reproduced byte-identically, renders pixel-identical. "BMW 3 groups -> 1" overstates it: the nose fairing (7.7k yellow px) stays yellow in both renders, because its highlight-washed albedo sits in the near-neutral Khaki group at chroma 10, below the rule's floor of 12; the highlight case is out of reach. About 7k of the red car's 33k merged px are the background Ford and floor reflections. Three small wrong-material merges on samples the veto cannot catch (brighter, more saturated candidates): a classic car's amber turn-signal lens (1,357 px) into the brass bumper group, a bicycle's sunglasses lens (488 px) into the jersey, a red hub piece (338 px) into the glove. Caveat wording fixed (the sneakers' Sand is a shoe edge band; the Ducati's Brown is the rear hub and an engine casting; the Sazabi's Army green is the LED-lit chin).

**Verdict.** Improves. **Cost.** No model, no dependency; about +0.9 s per image in the evalkit chain. **What it would take to ship.** `gplib.absorb_lit` as a step between `grouping.group_regions` and `refine.refine_groups` and inside `regroup_refined` (a regroup must reproduce the analysis), `LitParams(alb_tol 8, pho_tol 7, kC 2, mat_c_ratio 0.6, mat_l_margin 10)`, with two guards before shipping: a raw-albedo-chroma floor of 18 for candidates (the sneakers edge band) and the stack's photo-lightness veto (a candidate whose photo L is more than 35 below the anchor's is another material: the 133 px mirror case). One day.

### 4.8 `group_shiny` (S5, S4; the second complaint)

**What was tried.** Per-region features from the layers: positive-residual energy fraction, sensor-clipped share, the engine's glint share, a highlight share, photo-chroma fraction, hue stability, the median colour of the unclipped remainder (2 px inside the rim), a min-channel specular-free image (Tan and Ikeuchi), the log-luminance spread, shared boundaries. Two rules: (i) a bright, low-chroma region joins a touching paint group when its albedo chroma is < 0.95 of the paint's, albedo L >= 55, photo L >= 50, highlight share >= 0.15 and the remainder's hue is within 18 deg of the paint's with normalised chroma >= 10 and stability >= 0.6; (ii) a region >= 500 px is chrome (one locked group) when max(clip, glint) >= 0.25, albedo chroma <= 16, albedo L 25-78, remainder L <= 70, no stable hue, log-luminance spread >= 0.55. Sweeps against the material tags of four fixtures; renders; evalkit group maps; 13 live samples.

**What happened.** The targets exist in the features: the BMW's washed fairing region (59k px in the white Khaki group) has a remainder hue of 86 against the paint's 87 at chroma 50, the RX-78 shield facet 19 against 21; chrome that reflects the paint keeps the hue but not the chroma (2-16 vs 10-50). Residual energy is a lightness proxy, so brightness gates were needed; glossy white plastic shares chrome's clip and glint signature and had to be excluded by albedo L. On the tagged fixtures every chrome move is chrome (6 hits, 0 false) but recall is 6 of 29 (dark chrome that looks black, bright flat chrome that looks white); the two highlight moves are paint. Of the five shiny-in-white cases the references expose, two are fixed (BMW fairing region, RX-78 facet) and three are not (the Ducati frame highlight has no adjacency to its paint through the grey-albedo frame; its fork tube and footpeg bracket are chrome that looks white). Group-map purity 0.779 -> 0.790 on ours. The BMW render paints the nose and top-fairing highlight zones with the paint, as asked, but the stored region 60 is a mixed region (34 % upper fairing, 13 % front rim, 6 % disc, 4 % muffler) and moves whole: bleed 1,658 -> 6,668, interior dE 12.8 -> 19.0, navy tint and white speckles on both wheels and the disc; on a fresh BMW analysis the region does not exist and the highlight zones are already in the paint. Rule (ii) fires on dark glossy plastic (robot toy).

**Verifier corrections applied (major).** Rule (i)'s sample precision is overstated: of 14 highlight moves on the 13 samples about 6 are wrong (the street truck's two lit headlights into the cab yellow, a tan leather headrest into the yellow car's group, a 23k px wall / ceiling-beam region into the robot's Red, a grey plastic arm, a workbench clip) and 6-7 right (the sneakers' clog strap and rim, the Exia antenna edge, the RX-78 facet, a classic car's emblem wheel, a Sazabi decal if decals count: the rule cannot tell a decal from a highlight). The BMW render is a trade, specific to old labels. Rule (ii)'s false chrome extends to the Sazabi's black glossy joint, the classic car's dark hood top and three building-facade pieces on car_red_sports_2. The purity gain rests on 3 chrome moves on 6 images.

**Verdict.** Mixed. **Cost.** No model; about 0.5-0.9 s (features from the residual the decomposition already provides). **What it would take to ship.** Rule (i) only as an extension of `refine.absorb_washed` with the verifier's guards: a lamp / emission guard (both headlight moves have a blown core: a clipped-fraction ceiling, or "positive residual far above the touching paint's brightness"), a cap on the moved region's size relative to the paint group, a background exclusion for the candidate as well as the target, decal islands excluded; then re-run the 13 samples and report every move by eye. Rule (ii) as an advisory "chrome?" badge in the groups panel, never an automatic lock, until a material classifier raises its recall. Two days.

### 4.9 `bggroup` (S3, S4, S5, S2; the third complaint)

**What was tried.** BiRefNet_dynamic (MIT, revision `2803060`, fp16) as a foreground prior on 328 images; background rules on the fixed shipping partitions: B1 (the smallest set of groups covering 90 % of the border, regions not connected to the border dropped), B2 (regions with matte foreground <= 0.15 that touch the border set are backdrop, >= 0.6 are object, the two sides clustered separately, backdrop groups flagged), B3 (B2 plus a cut of any region with >= 20 % of its pixels on each side of the 0.5 contour), a decisiveness gate, a second-saliency pass, `drop=rest`; material cues on top: M1 (photo-dark pixels down-weighted in the medians), M2 (round 1's blend linkage, later with a lightness cap), M3 (a photo-L-spread and specular veto), M4 (van de Weijer's quasi-invariant edge ratio as a merge bridge or veto), M5 (MatSeg descriptors); the colour-family ground truth; fixture fragmentation and guard pairs; renders; the live pipeline on four images.

**What happened.** B1 flags every image but takes object groups that touch the border (leakage 25-47 %): rejected. B2 fixes the products: scored-part pixels inside background groups on the five product images 4.61 % -> 0.12 % (the Ducati 17.2 % -> 0.12 %: its rear wheel, tyre, swingarm and engine internals leave the background), coverage 0.59 -> 0.93, a flag on 23 of 23 samples (shipping 15), both RX-78 backdrop halves flagged, the robot toy's grey frame out of the granite floor, PACO leakage 13.6 % -> 5.1 %, PASCAL 18.3 % -> 1.1 %. It fails where the salient object is not the only subject: on the street scene BiRefNet picks the man and his barricade, so the truck and the left person become backdrop (57 % of that image's part pixels; pooled ours leakage rises 3.66 % -> 11.8 %); the gate does not detect this (every sample is >= 95 % decisive) and the second-saliency pass hallucinated an object in the Ducati's backdrop. B3's cut is a small partition gain (ASA and F@2 +0.002-0.016 on every set, about 4 % more regions). M2 is the best material cue (family F1 +2.8, the best fragmentation) but makes 3 BMW guard failures (gold fork top and mirror stalk into the paint); a 45 L cap keeps every gain and removes both (one non-paint guard remains: mirror stalk with fork upper). M1 helps F1 (+2.4) but merges the Ducati's carbon fender with the seat and fragments the Sazabi (0.77); M3 splits glossy paint (F1 0.691 -> 0.608); M4's invariant reads a saturation change (red paint to white decal) as a highlight (6 new BMW guard failures); M5 is neutral (+0.4) for a 532 MB model. Chosen (B2 + B3 + M2 cap 45, `drop=rest`): family F1 0.683 -> 0.722 (an oracle on these regions reaches 0.876: the partition caps it), purity 0.777 -> 0.788, fixtures RX-78 0.531 -> 1.000, red car 0.858 -> 0.996, Sazabi 0.941 -> 0.996, BMW 0.911 -> 0.938, Exia -0.0013. Renders on the stale fixture labels: the BMW nose one navy with the rest and bleed 24,888 -> 3,892, but the fixture's mixed nose-plus-highlights region carries the front disc, muffler and exhaust box into the paint (spill 1,473 -> 9,583 px); the live partition has no such region and its nose already groups with the paint. Live pipeline: Ducati 14 groups / 1 background -> 21 / 7 (floor flagged too), RX-78 13 / 1 -> 13 / 5, street 22 / 0 -> 23 / 16.

**Verifier corrections applied.** B3's cut alone regresses the BMW fixture 0.911 -> 0.636 (the cut turns the mixed region into a yellow piece that clusters as its own gold group) and only M2 repairs it: the cut and the blend distance are coupled. The disc / muffler spill also appears on the 23-sample partition of the BMW; the only evidence it vanishes is one live run. Runtime is 0.12-0.22 s per image, not 0.12. The partition gain is ASA and F@2 only. The Ducati render's bleed rises 20 % (9,577 -> 11,535). M1 helps F1 and was rejected for its wrong merges.

**Verdict.** Mixed; partial adopt (the matte background yes; of the material cues only M2 with its cap, or `absorb_lit` in its place). **Cost.** A third model (BiRefNet_dynamic, MIT): 2.3 GB allocated at 1536 px, 0.12-0.22 s per image, a few seconds to load from the local cache; grouping stays CPU at 0.3-0.6 s. **What it would take to ship.** `grouping.py`: `fg_prob` stored next to the layers (float16); the B2 split (`border_cover` 90 %, `f <= 0.15` backdrop, `f >= 0.6` object, separate `cluster_colors` runs); flags kept through refine by replacing the 35 % border rule in `_mark_background` with a pixel-majority-of-backdrop rule and a per-region backdrop flag in the job so regroup, merge and refine agree; the B3 cut in `pipeline._stage_regions` after `_recover_parts` (`split_on_matte`, share 0.2, threshold 0.5); missing weights keep the shipping rule; tests for the see-through hole, the lightness cap and flag persistence. Two to three days, plus the product switch in section 6.

## 6. What should go into the product

Ranked by verified gain per unit of cost and risk, with the owner's three complaints as the tie-breaker. Each item carries the criteria that decide whether it ships; every criterion is one the harness, the fixture renders or the existing regression crops can measure.

Ship with the raw-chroma floor (18) and the photo-lightness veto (35 L).
